# Retention Editing Standard

## Purpose

Every episode receives one retention pass after the evidence-backed script is complete and before visual timing is locked.

The goal is not to make the writing more sensational. The goal is to remove friction while preserving accuracy, nuance and the channel's calm intelligent tone.

## Required checks

### 1. Hook speed
- Establish the viewer's problem or tension immediately.
- State the core thesis early.
- Reach the first concrete payoff quickly.
- Avoid leading with research terminology before the viewer understands why it matters.

### 2. Concrete before abstract
Prefer:
- lived experience
- observable behavior
- contrast
- example
- question

Before:
- terminology
- study labels
- academic explanation

Research supports the claim; it should not routinely become the claim.

### 3. Compress repeated caveats
Preserve scientific caution, but do not repeat the same disclaimer structure in every section.

Prefer one compact qualification over:
claim -> caveat -> exception -> second caveat -> restatement.

### 4. Mini-payoffs
Each major section should contain at least one memorable line, contrast, question or practical inference.

### 5. Structural variation
Avoid giving every numbered section the same rhetorical shape. Alternate among:
- concrete example
- contrast
- question
- surprising correction
- research support
- concise takeaway

### 6. Word-count target
A retention pass should typically remove about 5-12% of words when the first complete draft is already strong.

Do not cut merely to hit a percentage. Preserve clarity, evidence and useful examples.

### 7. Narration-aware editing
For PocketTTS:
- keep synthesis units contextual rather than micro-segmented;
- use explicit semantic groups only when a chunk is rushed or exceeds the model's comfortable token range;
- do not use WPM-based time stretching;
- selectively regenerate only changed chunks.

### 8. Approval gate
Before locking storyboard timing:
1. generate the edited narration;
2. listen to the full episode sequentially;
3. verify voice stability, glitches, rhythm and engagement;
4. compare against the previous version when available;
5. only then lock visual timing.

## MLI-001 validation

The first full narration was about 8:02 and felt credible but somewhat academic and repetitive through the middle.

The retention pass reduced the script from 1197 to 1101 words (-8.0%). The revised narration is about 7:28. It reaches payoffs faster, reduces repeated caveats and makes Signs 3, 4 and 6 more direct without changing the seven-sign thesis or the evidence-backed caution.

This episode validates retention editing as a production stage.
