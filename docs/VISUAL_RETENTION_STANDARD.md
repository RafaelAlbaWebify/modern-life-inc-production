# Visual Retention Standard

## Priority

Visual choices are optimized for viewer retention first. Generation cost and production speed are secondary constraints.

## Modes

### HERO
Use for:
- section openers with emotional or behavioral importance;
- high-value payoff moments;
- moments where a human face/body interaction carries more information than a diagram;
- conclusion moments that should feel memorable.

Treatment:
- hyperrealistic cinematic photography;
- believable people and environments;
- strong focal composition;
- subtle practical amber lighting;
- no fantasy glow, particles, or magical light trails;
- overlays added later by the compositor.

### HYBRID
Use for:
- human behavior that benefits from explanatory overlays;
- contrasts between two behaviors;
- phone/message/conversation scenes;
- examples where the viewer should first see a believable human situation, then receive a graphic cue.

Treatment:
- hyperrealistic photographic base;
- clean negative space;
- restrained overlays, text or simple diagrams added separately.

### SYSTEM
Use for:
- abstract concepts;
- evidence cards;
- heuristics;
- convergence diagrams;
- short pattern interrupts;
- brand outro;
- moments where a diagram communicates faster than another human photograph.

## Retention rules

1. Avoid long runs of SYSTEM scenes when the narration is about human behavior.
2. Reintroduce human faces/body language regularly through the episode.
3. Prefer human imagery for lived behavior; prefer SYSTEM for abstraction.
4. Use SYSTEM as a pattern interrupt, not as the default because it is cheaper.
5. A HERO/HYBRID image should not sit completely static for its entire scene. Use subtle crop, push-in, reframing, overlays or staged reveals.
6. Avoid a repeated AI-slideshow feeling. Vary between close-ups, two-person scenes, environmental shots, phone interactions, split contrasts and diagrams.
7. The visual should reinforce the narration rather than merely illustrate the literal words.
8. Hyperrealism is the default photographic direction for Modern Life Inc.
9. Amber/orange is a practical lighting/accent language, not a fantasy effect.
10. If retention and generation cost conflict, retention wins.

## MLI-001 validation

Retention-first reclassification:
- HERO: 10
- HYBRID: 22
- SYSTEM: 24
- scenes with generated photographic bases: 32 of 56

The original plan had only 20 HERO/HYBRID scenes. The revised plan increases human/photographic presence in behavior-heavy sections and moves abstract moments such as waveforms and convergence back to SYSTEM.
