# MLI-001 — Research Brief
## Working title
Why Your Raise Still Doesn't Feel Like a Raise

## Core question
Why can somebody receive a nominal pay increase and still feel as though their standard of living barely improved?

## Evidence base
1. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Real Earnings, June 2026:
   - From June 2025 to June 2026, real average hourly earnings were essentially flat/slightly negative depending on the series/revision.
   - The useful editorial point is not that everybody became poorer; it is that nominal wage growth and inflation can nearly cancel each other out.

2. OECD Employment Outlook 2026:
   - Annual real wage growth averaged 2.2% across OECD countries in Q1 2026.
   - Real wages still had not recovered to early-2021 levels in a meaningful share of OECD countries.
   - Recovery was slowing compared with a year earlier.

3. Federal Reserve — Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025:
   - 73% of adults said they were doing okay financially or living comfortably.
   - Price increases remained the most commonly cited financial concern; more than 9 in 10 adults described price increases as at least a minor concern.

## Economic mechanisms to explain
- Nominal wage vs real wage.
- Inflation is an average; an individual's personal inflation basket can differ.
- Large fixed/semifixed costs (housing, insurance, debt service) can dominate the household budget.
- A raise is taxed at the margin, so gross percentage ≠ spendable percentage.
- Lifestyle creep can absorb part of a raise, but should not be used to blame viewers for structural cost increases.
- Debt interest costs can rise independently of consumer-price inflation.
- Feeling richer depends on disposable income after essentials, not the headline salary number.

## Claims to avoid
- Do not imply that a 7% raise always loses to inflation.
- Do not say all rents/food/insurance rose by the fictional percentages shown in the cartoon.
- Do not treat official CPI as a household's exact personal inflation rate.
- Do not present investment advice.
