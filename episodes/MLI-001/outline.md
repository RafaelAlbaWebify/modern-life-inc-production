# MLI-001 Narrative Outline

## Target duration
7–9 minutes.

## Hook
People are terrible at reading attraction because they look for one dramatic clue.

The useful question is not:
"Did they make eye contact?"

It is:
"Do several low-risk behaviors keep pointing in the same direction?"

## Opening thesis
Attraction is private, so no gesture can prove it.
But research does show that attraction often changes behavior.

The strongest clues are repeated, selective and reciprocal.

## Sign 1 — They keep finding reasons to be near you
Core mechanism: direct approach / proximity / talking.

## Sign 2 — Eye contact becomes mutual, not accidental
Core mechanism: shared gaze and mate choice.

## Sign 3 — You start moving and talking in sync
Core mechanism: mimicry, synchrony and conversational alignment.

## Sign 4 — The conversation gets more personal
Core mechanism: self-disclosure and liking.

## Sign 5 — They respond to *you*, not just the topic
Core mechanism: responsiveness.

## Sign 6 — The conversation starts to feel unusually easy
Core mechanism: language-style matching / mutual rhythm.

## Sign 7 — They create another opportunity to see or talk to you
Core mechanism: future contact and behavioral investment.

## Synthesis
The real signal is convergence.

Use the 3C heuristic:
- Consistency
- Contrast / selectivity
- Reciprocity

(If "Contrast" sounds too technical in script, use "Difference": do they behave differently with you than with others?)

## Counterpoint
Friendly, confident or expressive people can show many of these behaviors without romantic interest.
Shy people may show fewer.
Culture and context matter.

## Takeaway
One sign means almost nothing.
Several repeated signals, especially when they involve effort and future contact, are more informative.

The only way to know with confidence is direct communication.
