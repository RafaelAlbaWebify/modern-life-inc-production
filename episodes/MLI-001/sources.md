# MLI-001 Sources

1. Montoya, R. M., Kershaw, C., & Prosser, J. L. (2018). A meta-analytic investigation of the relation between interpersonal attraction and enacted behavior. *Psychological Bulletin*.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733622/
   - Use for proximity, talking, eye contact, smiling, laughter, mimicry, and rejection-threat caveat.

2. Collins, N. L., & Miller, L. C. (1994). Self-disclosure and liking: a meta-analytic review. *Psychological Bulletin*.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7809308/
   - Use for disclosure-liking reciprocity.

3. Willems, Y. E., Finkenauer, C., & Kerkhof, P. (2020). The role of disclosure in relationships. *Current Opinion in Psychology*.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31430615/
   - Use for relationship-development framing.

4. Laurenceau, J.-P., Barrett, L. F., & Pietromonaco, P. R. (1998). Intimacy as an interpersonal process.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599440/
   - Use for responsiveness and intimacy.

5. Prochazkova et al. (2024). Sharing and Receiving Eye-Contact Predicts Mate Choice After a 5-Minute Conversation.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10920202/
   - Use for mutual eye contact in speed dating.

6. Ireland, M. E., et al. (2011). Language style matching predicts relationship initiation and stability. *Psychological Science*.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21149854/
   - Use for conversational synchrony and future-contact outcome.

7. Pfaus, J. G., Safron, A., & Zakreski, E. (2023). Behavioral and brain synchrony during attraction, courtship, and sexual interaction.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37544764/
   - Use for synchrony as relationship-formation mechanism.

8. Joel et al. (2022). Initial impressions of compatibility and mate value predict later dating and romantic interest. *PNAS*.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322750/
   - Use for later messaging, dates and relationship initiation.

9. Human et al. (2015). Why Do You Make Us Feel Good? Correlates and Interpersonal Consequences of Affective Presence in Speed-dating.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25750481/
   - Use for responsiveness, positive affect and desire to meet again.

10. Li et al. (2009). An evolutionary perspective on humor: sexual selection or interest indication?
    - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19407005/
    - Optional support for humor dynamics; do not use to claim teasing proves attraction.
